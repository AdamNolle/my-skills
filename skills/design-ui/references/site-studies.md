# Twenty-two reference studies

Research date: **2026-09-17**. All 22 supplied URLs were visited in the Codex in-app browser. The accepted evidence contains desktop compositions, selected interaction states, and phone-size captures for six sites. These are visual and interaction samples, not exhaustive UX, accessibility, source-code, or performance audits.

The user's strongest whole-site preferences were Remy Shoots, Studio Loop, and Locomotive. Parakeet and Weird Folders were emphasized specifically for icons. They receive the deeper treatment below.

Desktop captures generally use 1280 × 720. Phone captures use 390 × 844 browser viewports, not real-device touch or performance tests. Animated frames are evidence of the sampled moment. Unreadable transitional captures and redundant loading states were excluded from the accepted set. Exact CSS samples for selected sites document declared computed values only; they do not establish the complete design system or asset licenses.

Each numbered capture below is a **successfully captured visual step**. The scope and risk notes state what was not verified. “Transfer” sections are original design recommendations inferred from the observations, not statements about the site's implementation. Font timings, frameworks, shaders, and performance results are not guessed.

## 01 · Daylight Computer

[Live source](https://daylightcomputer.com/) · Physical product

**The technology feels like an object that belongs in a human environment.**

**Visual anatomy.** The opening uses a nearly full-window photograph inside a softly rounded outer frame. A pale tablet rests in moss and vegetation; its angle breaks the page grid while the title stays left aligned. Warm orange actions are legible against the natural dark image. The navigation sits in its own pale surface at the upper right. A small film thumbnail and lower purchase action balance the composition. The mixture of clean sans text, occasional editorial serif, paper tones, and environmental light softens the technical subject.

**Observed behavior.** The inspected opening changed between a daylight screen and an amber-lit screen while preserving the product position and surrounding interface. This continuity makes a feature feel physical. The newsletter panel and secondary promotion compete with the otherwise calm composition.

**Transfer to new work.** For a new product, stage the object in its intended emotional context, then connect material or lighting changes to a real benefit. Keep a stable focal point and select photography whose quiet regions can carry text. A warm interface needs credible lighting and texture more than a beige palette.

**Risks and limits.** Do not copy the overlapping promotional density. Text over photography needs contrast checks across every scene, not just the chosen screenshot. No checkout or performance audit was performed.

**Inspection scope.** Desktop hero and alternate lighting state. Later pinned text was not accepted as visual evidence because it was too faint at the sampled scroll position.

## 02 · Electronic Materials Office

[Live source](https://electronicmaterialsoffice.com/) · Physical product

**Industrial restraint gains personality from one luminous accent and an unexpectedly elegant serif.**

**Visual anatomy.** A black keyboard spans almost the entire viewport on a black stage. Light catches edges and surfaces rather than revealing every detail equally. The large serif product name sits at the lower left; a compact reserve panel balances it on the right. A small red-orange light in the corner is echoed by the glowing action button and a colored hardware detail. Rounded dark macro-image panels continue the product study below.

**Observed behavior.** Scrolling moves from the overall keyboard to tight views of keycaps, perforations, and a projecting control. A slim translucent header retains brand and action. The sequence changes scale while preserving the same material language.

**Transfer to new work.** Use an overall view to establish recognition, then a few specific close-ups to show why the object is desirable. Align accent color with an actual product feature. Pair a characterful display face with plain explanatory text rather than making every element look technical.

**Risks and limits.** Near-black renders need careful highlight placement and a useful fallback image. Glow should identify the action, not become a border treatment on every surface. Purchase flow and motion fallback were not tested.

**Inspection scope.** Desktop opening and scrolled macro-detail sequence.

## 04 · Immersive Garden

[Live source](https://immersive-g.com/) · Immersive collection

**Luxury is expressed through quiet depth and atmosphere rather than visual density.**

**Visual anatomy.** The opening is a nearly white sculptural field with birds, insects, and organic forms that resemble shallow relief. Black serif copy runs across the middle; the studio mark, scroll instruction, menu, and sound control are sparse. Much of the composition is intentionally empty. **Observed behavior.** After the loading stage, the relief scene becomes visible. Scrolling brings a larger serif statement through the environment and begins the project sequence. The environment provides continuity while text changes scale. Sound is presented as an optional layer.

**Transfer to new work.** For an atmospheric brand, use one coherent environmental material, minimal interface chrome, and a small set of readable text positions. Build the static composition first. A proposed implementation could combine a semantic page with a decorative spatial layer and still-image fallback.

**Risks and limits.** The loading sequence and low-contrast material are real costs. Make content reachable if rendering fails, and avoid letting the atmospheric layer reduce text contrast. The exact renderer and animation implementation were not investigated.

## 07 · Remy Shoots

[Live source](https://www.remyshoots.co.za/) · Photographic instrument

**The portfolio behaves like a photographic viewing instrument, with images as the substance and controls as precision markings.**

**Visual anatomy.** On desktop, a charcoal field carries compact mono labels at the top and bottom. A colored central portrait is flanked by darker, desaturated neighbors. Red triangular pointers and ruler-like ticks repeatedly identify the active position. The brand is small because the photographs carry the identity. Grid and list modes reorganize the same collection: the grid fills the field with photographs, while the list leaves large black regions around a central image and spreads metadata horizontally. Sampled label CSS declared Roboto Mono at about 13.44px with tight line-height and tracking.

**Observed behavior.** The entry offers sound or a quiet path. Slider, grid, and list controls were exercised; the active project remained recognizable in the inspected transitions. The optional fisheye control introduced a curved, magnified treatment. Selecting the active photograph opened a white project view with adjacent vertical images, compact project identity, a counter, and the same tick-mark vocabulary. At 390px, the experience became a full-height portrait with a curved lower caption frame. The mobile menu opened as a right-aligned list over a blurred photograph.

**Transfer to new work.** The valuable mechanism is continuity: one collection, one active identity, several useful representations, one repeated framing language. A new gallery should derive all modes from one data model and preserve selection. Use a photographic subject whose crop still has power on a phone. Choose a few original framing details and repeat them; do not borrow the whole red-and-mono identity. Translate the same idea into an app as list/grid continuity and a clear selected-item inspector.

**Risks and limits.** The entry gate, custom image navigation, tiny labels, and optional distortion all add friction. Keep work reachable through ordinary links, make controls usable by keyboard and touch, and ensure a reduced-motion version preserves content. A custom renderer or exact transition timing cannot be established from these captures. The research did not verify every project, gesture mode, keyboard path, or assistive-technology behavior.

**Inspection scope.** Deep sample: desktop entry, three modes, fisheye, one project opening, mobile gallery, and mobile menu. This is the most extensively exercised gallery reference.

## 08 · Raycast

[Live source](https://www.raycast.com/) · Precision software

**A utility feels desirable when the promotional world and the actual interface share the same precision.**

**Visual anatomy.** The near-black opening uses a luminous red diagonal motif behind a centered, relatively simple sans headline. A wide translucent navigation panel sits above it. **Risks and limits.** Dark muted text and translucent layers need contrast checks. A looping demo should have a useful static poster. Do not mistake visible product media for tested app functionality.

## 09 · Studio Loop

[Live source](https://www.studioloop.com.br/) · Editorial studio

**Playfulness works because the objects, type, and spacing are art-directed as one composition.**

**Visual anatomy.** The opening places a moving diagonal collage of printed pieces, furniture, apparel, and other studio objects over a deep brown field. An orange script-like brand mark anchors the cluster. Small navigation remains conventional and quiet. The service section replaces objects with large type: sans and serif words mix within a line, underlines act as emphasis, small numbers add rhythm, and faint rules divide generous rows. The next captured section switches to an off-white field, a large editorial paragraph, mixed font emphasis, and scattered orange circular accents. Brown, orange, cream, and lavender create a more specific palette than generic black and white.

**Observed behavior.** Scrolling changes the dominant medium from collage to type to explanatory copy. The original object art and font files are not included for reuse. The full menu focus cycle, form submission, and complete case-study media sequence were not tested.

## 13 · Parakeet

[Live source](https://parakeet.co/) · Tactile icons

**The important reference is the distinction between memorable app identities and coherent utility glyph families.**

**Visual anatomy.** The page uses a saturated blue field, a translucent rounded navigation surface, a playful display face, and two illustrated portraits with layered colored halos. The app-icon gallery gives each square icon enough room to show material, silhouette, and internal depth. Examples vary from flat symbolic forms to carefully rendered objects. The glyph boards are more disciplined: each client family has a common visual weight, shape character, and limited palette. A visible grid lets differences in mass and alignment be compared.

**Observed behavior.** The inspected scroll moves from an introduction to app icons and then to glyph boards. At 390px, the portraits move above the text, the wordmark becomes a compact bird mark, and selected navigation remains visible. The first row of app icons stays large enough to inspect.

**Transfer to new work.** Design app identity and utility glyphs as separate systems. For a glyph family, compare silhouettes, optical weight, corner character, and padding together. For an app icon, choose one central metaphor and one consistent material story. Review actual-size exports rather than only large presentation art.

**Risks and limits.** Different client app icons are not one family to mix into a new interface. Large-gallery craftsmanship does not automatically survive at 16px. The bright background is a presentation choice, not a required palette for a productivity app. No assets were downloaded or licensed.

**Inspection scope.** Desktop introduction, app-icon gallery, client glyph boards, and phone opening.
