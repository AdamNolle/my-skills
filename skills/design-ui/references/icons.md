# Icon craft: Parakeet and Weird Folders

The user called these two sources out specifically for icons. Study their images in the [site studies](site-studies.md), not only their surrounding page design.

## Two different disciplines

**Parakeet's app icons** are miniature identities: a strong silhouette, memorable central metaphor, controlled depth, and a deliberate relationship with the outer shape. Its glyph boards are different: simplified symbols with a common optical weight and geometric language. Do not shrink a richly rendered app icon into a 16px toolbar glyph and expect it to work.

**Weird Folders** holds the folder metaphor recognizable while changing material, construction, and subject. Metal, paper, food, plastic, and electronics differ dramatically, yet the tab, overall mass, perspective, and catalog framing create kinship. Scratches, edges, thickness, and small labels make the objects tactile. The lesson is a coherent family with character, not random photographic detail.

## Write an icon brief

Specify the use before drawing: app identity, utility action, navigation category, file type, empty-state illustration, or collectible object. Define the smallest displayed size and background modes.

For a utility family, define:

- Coordinate grid, keyline, optical size, and outer padding.
- Filled or outlined construction, stroke weight, line caps, joins, and corner character.
- Common perspective, direction, and treatment of overlaps.
- Active, inactive, disabled, and selected variants.
- How labels and accessible names identify ambiguous actions.

For material icons, additionally define camera angle, light direction, softness of shadow, roughness, thickness, edge highlights, surface wear, and the amount of detail that survives downsampling. Decide which properties stay consistent and which may vary between items.

## Create and inspect a family

Prototype a small representative set: one simple form, one asymmetric form, one dense object, and one circular form. Review them together before expanding the collection. Equal bounding boxes do not create equal visual weight: optically correct circles, diagonals, and sparse silhouettes.

Inspect at actual intended sizes. Useful review scales might be 16/20/24px for glyphs and 32/64/128px for illustrative icons, adapted to the target platform. Check the light and dark background, grayscale silhouette, and selected state. If recognition depends on tiny text, a reflection, or a hairline, simplify the shape.

Use licensed vector assets or a coherent established library for utility glyphs. Use appropriate illustration, 3D, or image-generation tools for original app identity and material art. Request individual assets with consistent camera and light when possible. Avoid mixing emoji, incompatible stroke libraries, stock 3D objects, and one-off hand-drawn approximations in the same toolbar.

A material-asset prompt should specify a system, for example:

“Original folder-shaped icon for an audio library; recognizable tab and compact rectangular silhouette; matte charcoal polymer; inset speaker grille; soft upper-left light; slight front three-quarter camera; consistent baseline and contact shadow; clean isolated output suitable for dark and light surfaces; readable at 64px; no logo or text.”

This is an original design direction, not a request to reproduce a Weird Folders asset.

## Use the detail in the right place

Rich icons work well in onboarding, an app launcher, a category gallery, or a calm empty state. Dense tables, destructive actions, menus, and repeated controls benefit from simpler glyphs and explicit labels. Do not make every control a miniature illustration.

Parakeet's bright gallery background demonstrates diversity between client identities; it is not a single icon family to mix into a new app. Evaluate consistency within the chosen client glyph board. Weird Folders' distressed surfaces create personality, but the deliberately tiny, quiet captions should not become the default text treatment in a productivity interface.

Respect the source assets' rights. Screenshots are for research; do not extract and ship these icons without permission or the appropriate license.
